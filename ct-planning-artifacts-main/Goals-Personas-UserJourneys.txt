User Personas
1. Task/ Mission‑Driven Buyer

Short Description: A shopper who arrives with a clear goal: buy the correct tires fast, without distractions.

Entry Point: Start Tire Search (highest‑intent flow)

What Makes Them Different:
Wants the shortest path to fitment → results → checkout.
Has no interest in browsing or learning.
Extremely sensitive to steps, load time, and friction.
Measures success by speed and certainty.



2. Searcher / Problem Solver

Short Description: A user who begins by typing a direct keyword, size, or brand into search instead of navigating menus.

Entry Point: Global Search (query‑driven, fastest targeted access)

What Makes Them Different:
Trusts search boxes more than menus.
Wants intelligent suggestions and quick shortcuts.
Often knows something (size, brand, need) but needs confirmation.
Success depends on high search accuracy and intent detection.



3. Explorer / Category Browser

Short Description: A shopper who prefers to browse, compare, and educate themselves before deciding.

Entry Point: Shop Navigation (category‑based exploration)

What Makes Them Different:
Moves through filters, categories, and comparison pages.
Wants to understand options before committing.
May not know their size or brand preference yet.
Needs strong navigation, filters, and educational modules.


4. Deal Hunter / Promo‑Driven Buyer

Short Description:
A price‑sensitive shopper motivated by promotions, seasonal deals, or coupons.

Entry Point: Deals / Promotions (deal‑first journey)

What Makes Them Different:
Arrives from banners, email, or homepage promos.
Expects instant value, not a shopping tour.
Needs promos to auto‑route into fitment, not landing pages.
Abandons quickly if they can’t see whether it’s a valid deal


5. Brand Loyalist / Preference‑Anchored Buyer

Short Description: A shopper who trusts a specific brand (e.g., Michelin, Bridgestone) and starts from that brand.

Entry Point: Brand Entry (brand galleries or brand search)

What Makes Them Different:
Decision anchored in trust of a brand, not price or broad exploration.
Needs fitment validation before seeing SKUs, or trust is lost.
Responds well to brand‑specific value messaging.
Success depends on filtering SKUs to only what fits their vehicle.


6. Maintenance Seeker / Car‑Care Planner

Short Description: A user arriving to solve a service need — rotation, flat repair, or installation.

Entry Point: Services‑First (service menu → scheduling)

What Makes Them Different:
Primary intent is service, not tire shopping.
Must have one unified scheduling flow, not multiple tools.
Tires may be a secondary upsell opportunity.
Values convenience, nearest warehouse availability, and clarity.


7. Repeat Buyer / Owner

Short Description: A returning customer who already bought tires from Costco and wants an effortless reorder.

Entry Point: Ownership / Reorder (Buy Again, past purchase history)

What Makes Them Different:
Expects a frictionless 1‑ to 2‑step path.
Fitment auto‑validated from saved vehicle data.
High lifetime value if the experience is smooth.
Least tolerant of re‑entering information.
